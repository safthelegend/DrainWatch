        Every 15 minutes DrainWatch reads tide gauges, forecasts, weather alerts and 311 reports from free government and open-data sources. No API keys, no tracking. For each hazard, a short, tested set of rules scores how bad conditions will get and scales it by how exposed each neighborhood is, then names the result: Low, Elevated, High or Severe.

        risk = hazard (0–4) × exposure + alerts

        The flood map in the time machine

        Land is colored as flooded when it sits below the water level and connects to open water, on a ~110 m grid from USGS 3DEP elevation. The water level is the Battery's, so it is a citywide approximation. It ignores seawalls, drainage and how long the surge lasts. Checked against Sandy: the model floods about 54 square miles; the city's own estimate is 51.
