      Why this matters

      Climate change is already here. It just doesn't hit every block the same way.

      New York has 520 miles of shoreline, a century-old sewer system and neighborhoods with very different amounts of shade, air conditioning and clean air. DrainWatch turns live public data into a plain-language risk level for each one.

        How it works

        From public data to a risk level you can act on

        1. 1

          Live public data

          Every 15 minutes DrainWatch pulls tides, forecasts, alerts and 311 reports from free government and open-data sources. No API keys, no tracking.

          • 2

            A transparent risk model

            Each hazard gets a 0–4 score: how bad conditions will get, scaled by how exposed each neighborhood is. The rules are short, documented and covered by automated tests.

            Hazard 0–4 × Exposure by neighborhood + Alerts & reports
          • 3

            Answers for your block

            39 neighborhoods are rated Low, Elevated, High or Severe for the next 48 hours, and any address gets a report card for all four hazards.

          DrainWatch is an educational tool, not an official warning system. In an emergency, follow Notify NYC and the National Weather Service, and call 911 if you are in danger.
